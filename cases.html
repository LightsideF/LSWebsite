Personal · Bankruptcy
The Bankruptcy Order That Should Never Have Been Made
Mr. G — annulment
Bktcy Annulled £60k v £194k

“I knew exactly what a bankruptcy petition was. When I walked into that meeting on the Friday afternoon, I already understood what I was looking at. The hearing was four days away. HMRC were claiming £60,000.”

What We Did

Lightside secured an emergency adjournment of the bankruptcy petition hearing at four days’ notice. When the bankruptcy order was subsequently made, we applied for annulment on the grounds the order should never have been made — a basis that, if successful, removes the bankruptcy from all records entirely, preserving Mr. G’s FCA authorisation. HMRC contested and raised their demand from £60,000 to £194,000. We challenged the revised figure successfully. The annulment was granted on the basis of the original petition debt. The bankruptcy was annulled and his FCA registration preserved.
